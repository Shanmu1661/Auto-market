10 Critical Things Mechanics Check in a Pre-Purchase Inspection

Marcus Vance
• August 24, 2026 • 6 min read

A shiny exterior paint job and freshly detailed interior can hide thousands of dollars in mechanical wear or prior structural frame collision damage. When investing your hard-earned money in a used vehicle, a professional pre-purchase inspection (PPI) is the most valuable protection you have.

1. Paint Depth Metering for Hidden Collision Repairs

Factory paint on modern vehicles typically measures between 4.0 and 5.5 mils (100–140 microns) with consistent uniformity across all panels. When an ASE inspector waves an ultrasonic paint depth gauge over fenders and doors, a reading of 8.0+ mils indicates clear repainting. Readings exceeding 14.0 mils reveal body filler (bondo) underneath, often pointing to an undisclosed accident omitted from Carfax.

Mechanic's Insider Tip

Always check the manufacturer VIN stickers on the inside of the front fenders, hood, and door jambs. If a fender is missing its original tamper-proof VIN label, that panel has been replaced.
